# Judging Instructions

## Guide for BYUMS Business Plan Competition Judges

Thank you for your participation as a Judge for the BYU Management Society Business Plan Competition! We are excited to have your expertise and insight in assisting the entrepreneurs with whom we are working. As much as we would love to financially assist every participant in their businesses, we promise every participant that they will **at least** receive valuable feedback from our judges. Many previous contestants have used this feedback to grow their business and lift their local communities. To ensure that helpful feedback is given to every contestant, we have created guidelines to assist our judges throughout the judging process.

## Big Picture

The BYUMS Business Plan Competition is being offered to:

- Promote self-reliance and support existing businesses in the region.
- Enable in-country job creation.
- Help individuals develop leadership and management skills to lift themselves and their communities.

For this reason, it is critical that the businesses are judged based on their potential to be successful, because successful businesses will help local economies grow and enable even more people to work and live principles of self-reliance.

## Awareness of Judging Bias

Judges must be mindful of potential biases related to their experiences, countries, or preferences toward a particular contestant. It's crucial to approach the competition with an objective mindset. This will be difficult to do because every business tells a compelling story, and we want to help each one. **Despite the emotional aspect of judging, it is critical that the prize money goes to businesses that will benefit local communities in the long term through their continued and increased success.**

Please keep in mind that cultural or regional differences can significantly influence business practices. Africa is a diverse continent with varying levels of economic development across different regions. Judges should strive to be mindful of local economic conditions, cultural practices, and market-specific factors, as these can significantly impact business operations. Business models may focus on social entrepreneurship or community-driven initiatives that address regional challenges. With that in mind, Judges must also be willing to give critical feedback enabling the business to adapt and change in positive ways.

## Evaluating Business Plan and Presentation

While evaluating, Judges should mainly consider the quality of the business plan over the presentation. The major focus of the judging is on the plans the business has for future success, in addition to established practices that have generated success. Some contestants may lack experience in presenting or may face language barriers. Participants should not be penalized for language barriers or nervousness during the presentation. The focus should be on the business's potential for success.

**Consider the following:**

- Is this an existing business with strong potential for growth?
- Does the business plan anticipate potential business challenges and plan ways to resolve those challenges?
- How will the prize money impact the business's ability to grow?
- Will this business realistically grow to boost the local economy?

## Judging — Comprehensive Review

Judges are encouraged to review all business plans and presentation videos before scoring. This ensures a consistent and fair evaluation. Judging sequentially can lead to bias, as the Judge may score later presentations higher than previous ones because of familiarity with the content.

## Automatic Disqualifiers

The competition does not allow certain types of businesses, including:

- Multi-level marketing, network marketing, or pyramid schemes
- Get-rich-quick schemes
- Buyouts, real estate syndications, tax shelters
- Purchase of a franchise or licensing agreements for distribution in different geographical areas

Any usage of generative AI (Artificial Intelligence) to **create** content will be cause for disqualification. AI tools may be used to check grammar or to ensure clarity in writing, but they should not be used to create presentations or reports. If you suspect that a participant used AI tools to create their presentation, please make a note of it for Headquarters to review.

## Logistics

The rubric must be completed on the file the judges are sent, not on another document or program.

When returning the completed rubric, the judges must send the file as an attachment to management_society@byu.edu and CC Nicol Legakis (nlegakis@byu.edu).

## Feedback Criteria

All judges are required to provide thorough, positive feedback to each business they judge. While not all areas need to have a response, enough feedback should be given in each of the categories to be helpful to the business's future growth. Be aware that the feedback given will be sent to the applicants. This means the comments should be considerate and deliberate. **As a reminder, in most cases, your feedback is the only prize participants will bring home.** For this reason, the best feedback includes:

- Specific Successes and Areas for Improvements
- Professional Tone
- Written to an Audience (the Business Team)

### Guidelines and Key Questions to Ask Yourself

- Please write **positive and constructive** criticism. In most cases it is all the participants will take back with them.
- Don't use slang or local idioms. Use simple vocabulary, as English is not the first language for most participants.
- Remember that most of the participants in the competition do not have formal business degrees, so avoid business jargon or acronyms. When necessary, use simpler terminology.
- Write as if you are directly speaking to the participants, because they will be receiving your feedback.
- Check your spelling and grammar before emailing your feedback to us.
- Avoid one-liners as "complete" feedback.
- Why did you score this business lower? Explain.
- What is an idea that could boost their business plan and/or efforts?
- What did you want to see more/less of?
- What aspect of their plan, if emphasized, would lead to the greatest success?

### Helpful Feedback Examples

- The cause that you are working towards is fantastic! Education opens a lot of doors that cannot be opened otherwise. You have sound business practices and organization, as demonstrated by your financial records. You have used the money that you have well to maximize potential growth. The questions I have are mostly about your competition. Are there other schools that take students from your potential group? What are some of your risk factors, and what gives you an edge over other schools? You mentioned the superior quality of your education, but how do you measure that? I think taking some time to consider ways your business stands out and then creating programs to emphasize that would pay major dividends in the future. Emphasize how unique your school is compared to others, specifically in ways that you can provide better quality services to your customers.
- Your business model is good and designed to be successful in the long term. You have a good direction as a company and are clearly working hard to get there. You had a good description of broad economic pain points; I would recommend narrowing the focus to 1-2 problems your service can solve. I also recommend highlighting the success stories of your clients in your marketing pitch. I would like to better understand how your service generates revenue on a unit basis. Your market size is defined as the entire Nigerian population—this is likely too broad to be actionable. A narrower definition (based on geography, demographics, etc.) will allow you to understand and attract potential customers more effectively. There is no description of competitors in your space; what makes you different? The financials provided appear to be aspirational. It would be good to understand prior results. Other questions to consider: What unit sales would be required to reach the targeted revenue figures? What is your probability of meeting those targets, of reaching 80%? 60%?
- The slide quality was strong—use of bullet points and graphics that point the audience in an effective way. I understood your business based on what you shared and what I read. However, your competitive advantage(s) were not well explained. Remember that price alone is not a competitive advantage. You could bundle low cost with another strategy, like faster time to completion. How are you different from your competitors? Are there only 2 people in your management team? Who are your general contractors, and what is their skill level? Are your construction techniques superior to your competitors, and if so, how? What is the feedback you have received from customers? Your financial analysis could have been more detailed in the video. To save time, skip the long introduction and make the conclusion shorter. It was helpful to see financials in tabular form over time on the slide deck—wish you could have shown a version of this in the video.

### Unhelpful Feedback Examples

- Thank you for using the money to help others.
- No local market information was provided.
- Good job!
- It would have helped if you had experience in marketing, but great job.
- I would like to understand your actual market. I know it's around 50% of the population, but how many people is that? Also, what does competition look like, and how do you differentiate? **[This is helpful, but it could use some comments about what they did well in their presentation (strengths).]**
- Your ROI doesn't make sense, but the rest of the plan is good. **[ROI is a term that may not be commonly understood among those without formal business training.]**
- I like this idea, and I think it could be a successful business. **[While this comment is positive, it does not give constructive feedback for the participant to consider.]**
