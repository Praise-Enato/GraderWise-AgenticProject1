# What "good" looks like, section by section

Generic reference material describing strong vs weak treatment of each part of a
business plan. No applicant data. Use it to ground scoring in the intent of each
criterion, not just its label.

## Problem & solution
- Good: a specific, real customer pain, evidenced; a solution that directly resolves it.
- Weak: a broad societal theme; a solution loosely related to the stated problem.

## Business & model
- Good: clear what the business does and its stage; a concrete revenue model with
  pricing and unit economics.
- Weak: mission statements with no explanation of how money is actually made.

## Products & services
- Good: a concrete offering, a customer-specific value proposition, a real
  differentiator that is hard to copy.
- Weak: features listed with no "why the customer chooses this".

## Market
- Good: a specific segment, evidence of size and demand, named competitors with
  a strengths/weaknesses read. (See market_sizing.md.)
- Weak: "everyone is a customer", "no competition", no demand evidence.

## Marketing & sales
- Good: a concrete customer-acquisition plan, defined sales channels, justified
  pricing, specific promotion tactics.
- Weak: "we will advertise on social media" with no plan, price, or channel.

## Operations
- Good: how the product/service is produced or delivered repeatably; location,
  facilities, capacity; key suppliers and logistics.
- Weak: no account of how the thing actually gets made or delivered.

## Management & team
- Good: named team, clear roles, relevant expertise, and evidence they can execute
  (track record, advisors, early traction).
- Weak: names with no roles or relevance; no basis for confidence in execution.

## Financials
- Good: itemized startup costs, revenue projections with a basis, an expense
  breakdown, a profitability/break-even analysis, and internally consistent
  figures. (See financial_credibility.md.)
- Weak: round aspirational numbers, no costs, inconsistent or impossible figures.

## Growth & risk
- Good: a credible path to scale; honest, specific risks WITH mitigations.
- Weak: "we will expand everywhere"; "there are no risks". The risk bar is high —
  a vague or absent risk section should score low.

## Scoring disposition
- Use the full range: fully-met criteria earn full marks; absent or unsupported
  criteria earn 0. Do not cluster at the safe middle.
- Do not award "pity points" to be kind. Adjacent-but-different content is not credit.
- Judge viability and substance over polish, presentation, or language fluency.
- Weigh the business within its own local market context; do not penalize a plan
  for operating in a developing economy.
