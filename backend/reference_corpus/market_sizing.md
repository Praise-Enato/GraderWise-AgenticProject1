# Market sizing and analysis

Generic reference material for evaluating the market section of ANY business plan.
No applicant data.

## TAM / SAM / SOM
- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: total demand if you captured 100% of every
  possible buyer. The biggest, most theoretical number.
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**: the portion of TAM your product and
  channels can actually serve (segment, geography, price point).
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**: the realistic share you can win in a
  defined period, given competition and capacity.
A strong plan narrows from TAM to a realistic SOM; a weak plan quotes only a giant
TAM and implies it will capture all of it.

## Top-down vs bottom-up
- **Top-down**: start from a big published market figure and take a percentage.
  Easy to inflate; a "1% of a huge market" claim is a warning sign on its own.
- **Bottom-up**: build from units — number of target customers × purchase frequency
  × price. More credible because every assumption is visible and checkable.
Prefer plans that show a bottom-up build or at least defensible assumptions.

## What good market analysis contains
- A **specific target customer**, not "everyone" or "all of the country".
- **Evidence of demand**: data, trends, or credible first-hand observation.
- **Competitors named**, including indirect competition and the status quo
  ("what people do today instead").
- **Competitor strengths and weaknesses**, from which the plan's own edge follows.

## Common mistakes (score low)
- **"The market is everyone / all of [large region]."** Too broad to act on.
- **"There is no competition."** Almost never true; usually means the team hasn't
  looked, or the problem isn't real. Treat as a red flag.
- **A giant TAM with no path to a reachable segment.**
- **Demand asserted with no evidence** of any kind.
- **Competitor list with no analysis** of how this business will win against them.
