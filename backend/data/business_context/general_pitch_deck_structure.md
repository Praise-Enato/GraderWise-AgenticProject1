# Standard Pitch Deck Structure & Evaluation Guide

## The Ideal Pitch Deck Structure (10-15 Slides)

### Slide 1: Title
- Company name and logo.
- One-line description (elevator pitch in under 10 words).
- Founder name(s) and contact information.
- Evaluation: Is the one-liner clear and compelling? Can you understand what the company does in 5 seconds?

### Slide 2: Problem
- What specific pain point does the target customer experience?
- How is the problem currently being solved (or not solved)?
- Quantify the problem: cost, time wasted, revenue lost, lives affected.
- Evaluation benchmark: The problem should be specific enough to pass the "hair on fire" test. Vague problems like "communication is hard" score low. Specific problems like "mid-size construction firms lose $50K/year in project delays due to manual scheduling" score high.

### Slide 3: Solution
- How does your product solve the problem?
- What is the core value proposition?
- Keep it simple — one key differentiator, not ten features.
- Evaluation benchmark: Can you explain the solution in one sentence? If it takes a paragraph, it's too complex.

### Slide 4: Product / Demo
- Screenshots, mockups, or demo of the product.
- Show the user experience, not architecture diagrams.
- Evaluation benchmark: Does the product look real and functional? Is the UI professional?

### Slide 5: Market Opportunity (TAM/SAM/SOM)
- Bottom-up market sizing with sources.
- TAM, SAM, SOM clearly defined and calculated.
- Market growth rate and trends.
- Evaluation benchmark: Is the TAM over $1B (for venture-scale)? Is the methodology bottom-up? Are sources cited?

### Slide 6: Business Model
- How does the company make money?
- Pricing strategy (subscription tiers, usage-based, transaction fees, etc.).
- Unit economics: CAC, LTV, gross margin.
- Evaluation benchmark: Is the revenue model clear within 30 seconds? Is LTV:CAC ratio at least 3:1? Are margins healthy for the industry (SaaS: 70%+, marketplace: 60%+)?

### Slide 7: Traction
- Revenue, users, growth rate, partnerships, pilots.
- Week-over-week or month-over-month growth metrics.
- Customer testimonials or case studies.
- Evaluation benchmark: Is there measurable traction? Growth rate of 5-7% week-over-week is strong. Even pre-revenue startups should show letters of intent, waitlists, or pilot customers.

### Slide 8: Competitive Landscape
- Positioning matrix (2x2 or feature comparison table).
- Key competitors identified with honest assessment.
- Clear differentiation and defensible advantages.
- Evaluation benchmark: Never claim "no competition." Are obvious competitors acknowledged? Is differentiation specific and defensible? Is there a moat (network effects, data, switching costs, brand)?

### Slide 9: Team
- Founders and key team members.
- Relevant domain experience and track record.
- Complementary skill sets (technical + business + domain).
- Evaluation benchmark: Does the team have founder-market fit? Relevant exits or industry experience? Are critical roles filled (CTO for tech product, sales leader for B2B)?

### Slide 10: Financial Projections
- 3-5 year revenue, expense, and profitability projections.
- Key assumptions clearly stated.
- Path to break-even or profitability.
- Evaluation benchmark: Are growth rates realistic (not >100% MoM sustained)? Are assumptions stated and reasonable? Is there a clear path to profitability?

### Slide 11: The Ask
- Funding amount being raised.
- Use of funds breakdown (product: X%, sales: Y%, ops: Z%).
- Key milestones the funding will achieve.
- Expected runway from this raise.
- Evaluation benchmark: Is the ask amount justified by the use of funds? Do the milestones make sense? Is the runway at least 18 months?

## Common Presentation Quality Issues

### Design Red Flags
- Walls of text with no visuals — pitch decks should be visual, not text documents.
- Inconsistent formatting, fonts, or color schemes.
- More than 15 slides — investors lose attention.
- No data visualization — charts and graphs are expected for financial data.
- Low-resolution images or screenshots.

### Narrative Red Flags
- Starting with the solution instead of the problem.
- No clear storyline or logical flow between slides.
- Burying the most important information in later slides.
- Using jargon or acronyms without definition.
- Missing a clear call to action (the ask).

### Content Red Flags
- No mention of competition or competitive differentiation.
- Financial projections without stated assumptions.
- Team slide with no relevant experience listed.
- Traction slide with vanity metrics (downloads, page views) instead of engagement or revenue.
- Market size without methodology or sources.
