# Financial Ratios & Benchmarks for Business Plan Evaluation

## Unit Economics Definitions

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend / Number of New Customers Acquired
- Includes: advertising, sales salaries, marketing tools, content creation costs.
- Benchmark SaaS CAC: $200-$500 for SMB, $5,000-$50,000+ for enterprise.
- CAC should decrease over time as brand awareness grows and referral channels mature.

### Lifetime Value (LTV)
- LTV = Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) x Gross Margin % x Average Customer Lifespan (months or years)
- Alternative formula: LTV = ARPU x Gross Margin % / Monthly Churn Rate
- Benchmark: SaaS companies typically see LTV of $1,000-$10,000 for SMB, $50,000-$500,000+ for enterprise.

### LTV:CAC Ratio
- The critical health metric for any subscription or recurring revenue business.
- **Healthy ratio: 3:1 or higher** (LTV is at least 3x the CAC).
- Ratio below 1:1 means you're losing money on every customer — unsustainable.
- Ratio of 1:1 to 3:1 means the business model needs improvement.
- Ratio above 5:1 may indicate underinvestment in growth — you could acquire customers faster.
- Benchmark: Top-performing SaaS companies achieve LTV:CAC of 3:1 to 5:1.

### CAC Payback Period
- CAC Payback = CAC / (Monthly Revenue per Customer x Gross Margin %)
- Benchmark: Under 12 months is healthy for SaaS. Under 18 months is acceptable for enterprise.
- Payback over 24 months is a red flag for most business models.

## Revenue Metrics

### Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)
- ARR = Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) x 12
- Only includes recurring/subscription revenue — excludes one-time fees, professional services.
- Used as the primary valuation metric for SaaS businesses.
- Benchmark growth rates by stage:
  - Pre-seed to Seed: 2x-3x year-over-year growth expected.
  - Seed to Series A: 2x-3x YoY ($1M-$3M ARR typical for Series A).
  - Series A to B: 2x-2.5x YoY ($5M-$15M ARR typical for Series B).
  - Series B to C: 1.5x-2x YoY.

### Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)
- MRR = Number of Paying Customers x Average Revenue Per Account
- Track: New MRR, Expansion MRR, Churned MRR, Net New MRR.
- Net Revenue Retention above 100% means existing customers are expanding faster than churning.
- Benchmark: Best-in-class SaaS companies have Net Revenue Retention of 120-140%.

### Churn Rate
- Monthly churn = Customers lost in month / Customers at start of month.
- Benchmark: SaaS monthly churn should be under 5% (ideally under 2% for enterprise).
- Annual churn above 30% is a serious red flag.
- Negative net revenue churn (expansion > churn) is the gold standard.

## Profitability Metrics

### Gross Margin
- Gross Margin = (Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue x 100%
- Benchmark by industry:
  - SaaS/Software: 70-85% (hosting, support costs)
  - Marketplace: 60-70% (payment processing, trust & safety)
  - E-commerce: 30-50% (inventory, shipping, fulfillment)
  - Hardware: 25-40% (manufacturing, materials)
  - Services/consulting: 50-60% (labor costs)

### Burn Rate & Runway
- Monthly Burn Rate = Total Monthly Operating Expenses - Monthly Revenue
- Runway = Cash in Bank / Monthly Burn Rate
- Benchmark: Startups should maintain at least 12-18 months of runway.
- Fundraise when you have 6+ months of runway remaining.

### Break-even Point
- Break-even Revenue = Fixed Costs / Gross Margin %
- Most venture-backed startups are not expected to be profitable in the first 2-3 years.
- However, a clear path to profitability must be demonstrated.

## Financial Projection Red Flags
- Revenue growing faster than 100% month-over-month sustained for more than 6 months.
- No cost of goods sold (COGS) line item — suggests the founder hasn't thought about delivery costs.
- Marketing spend at 0% of revenue — unrealistic customer acquisition assumption.
- Employee count staying flat while revenue grows 10x — operations don't scale magically.
- No mention of cash flow or working capital needs.
- Projections that show profitability in year 1 for a pre-revenue startup.
